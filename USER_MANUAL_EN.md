## 1. About Daisy

Daisy is a voice companion designed to make everyday information and spoken services easier to access. You can talk naturally to Daisy to:

- Have a conversation or ask general questions.
- Check the weather.
- Hear current news and open a story in more detail.
- Find and play podcasts.
- Browse and read available books.
- Create, review, and remove reminders.
- Continue a conversation without pressing the button after every reply.

Daisy requires power and a working Wi-Fi connection for most voice features.

## 2. Important Safety Information

### Emergency situations

Daisy is not an emergency service and cannot replace a telephone, doctor, caregiver, or emergency alarm.

- In New Zealand, call **111** for an ambulance, police, or fire emergency.
- In another country, call the local emergency number.
- Do not wait for Daisy if someone has chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, signs of a stroke, a serious fall, heavy bleeding, loss of consciousness, or another urgent condition.

### Medical and other important advice

Daisy may provide general information, but its answers can be incomplete or incorrect. Always confirm important medical, legal, or financial information with a qualified professional.

Take medicines only as directed by a healthcare professional. Do not change a dose because of a voice assistant response.

### Electrical safety

- Use the power supply and charging equipment provided or approved for the device.
- Keep the device and charger dry.
- Do not use damaged cables or connectors.
- Keep the device away from excessive heat, flames, sinks, baths, and other sources of water.
- If the device becomes unusually hot, smells unusual, or appears damaged, disconnect it and contact the device provider.

## 3. Device Overview

The exact position of controls may vary by hardware version.

### Display

The display shows Daisy's face, operating status, temporary messages, and a battery icon. Facial expressions indicate whether Daisy is listening, thinking, speaking, or waiting.

### Microphone

The built-in microphone listens after Daisy is awakened. Speak from nearby in a normal, clear voice.

### Speaker

Daisy's replies, books, podcasts, and reminders are played through the built-in speaker.

### Wake button

Use the wake button to begin speaking when you do not want to use the wake phrase. Pressing it while Daisy is speaking intentionally interrupts the current reply and starts a new listening session.

### Battery indicator

The battery icon contains five segments. Each segment represents approximately 20 percent of the available charge.

## 4. First-Time Setup and Activation

### Step 1: Connect power

Connect the device to its approved power supply and turn it on. Leave it connected during initial setup.

### Step 2: Connect to Wi-Fi

The device must be connected to the configured Wi-Fi network. If Wi-Fi has not been configured, follow the setup instructions supplied with the device or ask the family member, administrator, or provider who installed it.

### Step 3: Activate the device

When a new or reset device connects for the first time, it may display a six-digit activation code.

1. Note the code shown on the device.
2. On a phone or computer, open the activation page supplied by the device provider.
3. Enter the six-digit code and complete the requested account or device details.
4. Keep the device powered and connected while it checks the activation status.
5. Wait for the activation-success message.

The activation code is valid for 24 hours. If it expires, restart the activation process to obtain a new code.

### Step 4: Test Daisy

Say **"Hi Daisy"**, wait for the greeting or listening indication, and then say:

> What can you do?

If Daisy replies, setup is complete.

## 5. Starting and Ending a Conversation

### Wake Daisy by voice

1. Say **"Hi Daisy"** clearly.
2. Wait for the greeting and listening face.
3. Say your request.

### Wake Daisy with the button

1. Press the wake button once.
2. Wait for the start prompt or listening face.
3. Say your request.

### Continue naturally

After Daisy finishes speaking, it normally returns to listening automatically. You can ask a follow-up question without repeating the wake phrase.

### Interrupt a reply

Press the wake button while Daisy is speaking to stop the current audio and ask something else. Because this is an intentional interruption, the sentence being played will end immediately and may sound incomplete.

Wait until Daisy is listening before beginning the new request.

### End the conversation

Say one of the following:

- "Goodbye."
- "That's all."
- "Stop listening."
- "Thank you, goodbye."

Daisy returns to idle mode and waits for the next wake phrase or button press.

### Speaking tips

- Speak in complete, short sentences.
- Use a normal volume and pace.
- Reduce television, radio, or nearby conversation where possible.
- Wait for the listening indication before speaking.
- State dates and times clearly, including AM or PM when needed.
- If Daisy misunderstands, rephrase the request instead of repeating it more loudly.

## 6. Everyday Voice Commands

You can use natural language. The following are examples, not fixed phrases:

- "What day is it today?"
- "Tell me something interesting."
- "Can we talk about gardening?"
- "Explain that more simply."
- "Please say that again."
- "What can you do for me today?"

Some requests require current online information and may take several seconds. Daisy may tell you that it is searching while you wait.

## 7. Weather

Ask for a forecast by naming a place and, when relevant, a day.

Examples:

- "What is the weather today?"
- "What is the Auckland forecast tomorrow?"
- "Will it rain in Wellington this afternoon?"
- "What is the weather for Christchurch this weekend?"

For New Zealand locations, Daisy uses current forecast information from the configured weather service. Forecasts can change, so check official warnings during severe weather.

## 8. News

### Start the news

Examples:

- "Tell me the latest news."
- "Read the New Zealand news."
- "What's in the news today?"

Daisy reads a headline or summary and then gives a spoken menu.

### News menu

- Say **"One"** to read the story in more detail.
- Say **"Two"** for the next story.
- Say **"Three"** to exit the news.

While a long story is being read, the menu may change to:

- Say **"One"** to continue.
- Say **"Two"** for the next story.
- Say **"Three"** to exit the news.

You can also say natural commands such as "next news" or "exit news."

News availability depends on the online source and network connection. Reports may be updated after Daisy reads them.

## 9. Podcasts

### Find podcasts

Examples:

- "Show me the latest podcasts."
- "Find a New Zealand news podcast."
- "Play The Front Page podcast."

Daisy may read an episode title and position in the result list.

### Podcast menu

- Say **"One"** to play the episode.
- Say **"Two"** for the next episode.
- Say **"Three"** to exit podcasts.

You can also say "play," "next episode," or "exit podcasts."

### During playback

Press the wake button if you need to stop playback and make a new request. Podcast availability and audio quality depend on the publisher, network connection, and source feed.

## 10. Books and DAISY Reading

Daisy can present books made available by the administrator and supported online book sources.

### Browse books

Examples:

- "Show me the book list."
- "What books are available?"
- "Read me a book."

Daisy reads numbered titles. Choose a book by saying its number or title. In the book-list menu, you can also say:

- "Next list" to hear more titles.
- "Exit book list" to close the list.

When asked for a number, say a number from one to ten for the currently announced list.

### Reading controls

At the end of a reading section, Daisy provides these controls:

- Say **"One"** to continue.
- Say **"Two"** for the next page.
- Say **"Three"** to go to a page.
- Say **"Four"** to exit and save your place.

After choosing "go to a page," say the requested page number clearly.

### Resume reading

When you exit and save, Daisy stores your reading position where supported. Later, ask to continue or resume the book.

Examples:

- "Continue reading my book."
- "Resume where I stopped."
- "Go to page twenty."

Book formatting, page numbering, and navigation depend on the source edition.

## 11. Reminders

### Create a reminder

State the task and time in one sentence.

Examples:

- "Remind me to see my doctor tomorrow at 9 AM."
- "Remind me to take my medicine at 7 PM."
- "Remind me on Friday morning to call Sarah."

Daisy saves the reminder before confirming it. A successful confirmation includes the task and scheduled time. Daisy then asks what it can do for you today.

If the date or time is unclear, Daisy may ask a follow-up question. Listen carefully to the confirmed date, time, and AM/PM. Correct it immediately if it is wrong.

### Review reminders

Examples:

- "What reminders do I have?"
- "List my reminders."
- "Do I have a reminder tomorrow?"

### Delete a reminder

Examples:

- "Delete my doctor reminder."
- "Cancel the reminder for tomorrow at 9 AM."

If more than one reminder matches, Daisy may ask which one to remove.

### Receiving a reminder

At the scheduled time, the device displays and speaks the reminder. For the best reliability:

- Keep the device powered on.
- Keep it connected to Wi-Fi.
- Keep the speaker unobstructed and the volume audible.
- Do not rely on Daisy as the only reminder for critical medication, appointments, or safety tasks.

Reminders are stored by the service and synchronized with the device after reconnection. A reminder that becomes due while the device is powered off or disconnected may not be heard exactly at the scheduled time.

## 12. Display and Battery Status

### Face and operating state

- **Idle:** Daisy is waiting for the wake phrase or button.
- **Listening:** Daisy is ready for your request.
- **Thinking:** Daisy is processing the request or retrieving information.
- **Speaking:** Audio is playing through the speaker.

### Battery icon

The five battery segments indicate the approximate charge:

| Segments | Approximate charge |
|---|---:|
| 5 | 81-100% |
| 4 | 61-80% |
| 3 | 41-60% |
| 2 | 21-40% |
| 1 | 1-20% |

At low charge, the remaining segment is shown in red.

### Low-battery message

When a valid low-battery reading is detected, the display shows:

> LOW BATTERY  
> Please charge

The message is temporary and may be shown again if the device remains low. Charge the device promptly.

The low-battery message should agree with the battery icon. If the message appears while the icon shows four or five segments:

1. Connect the approved charger.
2. Restart the device after it has charged for a short time.
3. If the mismatch continues, contact the device provider because the battery sensor or calibration may need attention.

## 13. Connectivity and Automatic Updates

### Wi-Fi connection

Daisy needs Wi-Fi for speech recognition, online information, books, podcasts, reminders, and most replies. If the network is temporarily unavailable, wait for it to reconnect and try again.

### Secure device access

The device uses a secure activation credential to connect to the HiLily service. It normally renews this credential automatically. No regular user action is required.

If the device repeatedly shows an activation message or cannot reconnect after a restart, ask the family member, administrator, or provider to reactivate it.

### Software updates

Updates may change wording, available sources, or minor menu behavior. Keep the device powered and connected when an update is being installed. Do not disconnect power during a confirmed update process.

## 14. Privacy and Responsible Use

- Voice requests are sent to online services for recognition and response generation.
- Conversation information may be used by the configured service to provide continuity and personalized assistance.
- Do not speak passwords, bank card numbers, security codes, or other secrets to Daisy.
- Avoid discussing another person's private information without permission.
- Daisy can make mistakes. Confirm important names, dates, addresses, appointments, and instructions.
- Ask the device provider or administrator about account access, stored information, and local privacy settings.

## 15. Cleaning and Care

1. Disconnect power before cleaning.
2. Wipe the exterior with a soft, dry cloth.
3. Do not spray cleaner directly onto the device.
4. Do not allow liquid into the microphone, speaker, display, buttons, or connectors.
5. Keep the microphone and speaker openings clear.
6. Place the device on a stable surface with space around it.
7. Protect it from direct sunlight, heaters, and extreme temperatures.

## 16. Troubleshooting

### Daisy does not wake when you say "Hi Daisy"

- Move closer and say the phrase clearly.
- Reduce nearby television, music, or conversation.
- Check that the device has power.
- Press the wake button once and try speaking.
- Restart the device if it remains unresponsive.

### Daisy wakes but does not understand

- Wait until the listening indication appears.
- Speak in one short, complete sentence.
- Include a place, date, or time when relevant.
- Rephrase the request.
- Move the device away from loud appliances or speakers.

### Daisy says, "I did not catch that"

The speech service did not receive a clear request. Wait for Daisy to listen again, then repeat the request more slowly in a quieter environment.

### Daisy remains in listening mode

Background sound may be keeping the microphone active. Move the device to a quieter location, say "goodbye," or restart it if the condition continues.

### A spoken sentence ends suddenly

The wake button stops current playback when pressed. This is normal during an intentional interruption. Avoid pressing the button until Daisy finishes unless you want to interrupt.

If audio stops without a button press:

- Check Wi-Fi stability.
- Keep the device near the Wi-Fi access point for a test.
- Restart the device.
- Contact the provider if replies are repeatedly cut off.

### There is a long pause before a reply

Some searches, books, podcasts, or complex questions take longer. Wait while the thinking indication is displayed. If nothing happens after a reasonable period, press the wake button and make a shorter request.

### Audio is unclear or broken

- Check the Wi-Fi signal.
- Keep the speaker opening clear.
- Move the device away from interference and restart it.
- Try a short question to determine whether the issue affects all audio or only one podcast or book source.

### The battery icon and warning disagree

Charge and restart the device. If the warning returns while the icon still shows a high charge, contact the provider for battery-sensor inspection or calibration.

### A reminder did not play

- Confirm that the reminder was successfully saved.
- Ask Daisy to list current reminders.
- Check that the date, time, and AM/PM are correct.
- Make sure the device is powered, online, and audible at the scheduled time.
- Use a second alarm for critical reminders.

### The device shows an activation code again

Complete activation using the provider's activation page. If the device was already activated, restart it once and contact the administrator if the code returns.

### Daisy cannot load news, weather, a podcast, or a book

The network or content provider may be temporarily unavailable. Check Wi-Fi and try again later. Some individual items may be removed or unavailable from their publisher.

### When to contact support

Contact the device provider, family administrator, or installer when:

- The device will not power on.
- Charging equipment or the device is damaged.
- Activation repeatedly fails.
- Battery readings remain inconsistent after charging and restarting.
- Voice replies are repeatedly incomplete without interruption.
- The device cannot connect even though other devices use the same Wi-Fi successfully.

Provide the approximate time of the problem and describe what was displayed or spoken. This helps the administrator find the relevant diagnostic record.
